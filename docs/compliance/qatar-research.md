## Q1 Tax
### What the sources say
- GTA site lists these tax types: Global Minimum Tax, Income Tax, Capital Gains Tax, Withholding Tax, Excise Tax. VAT is not listed. Dhareeba is the tax e-portal. VERIFIED. https://www.gta.gov.qa/en (2026-11-18)
- Exactly what the sources say on VAT: PwC Tax Summaries (reviewed 2026-09-17) says Qatar has no VAT or sales tax and a GCC-wide VAT is expected "in the near future" at about 5%. A July 2026 adviser guide says no launch date was confirmed. A search-engine summary of another source claims the draft e-invoicing law is "preparation for VAT"; EY's note on the draft law does not mention VAT. No official VAT law or date was found. UNVERIFIED. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/qatar/corporate/other-taxes ; https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/qatar.md ; https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/qatar-approves-draft-e-invoicing-law-and-implementing-regulations (all 2026-11-18)
- CIT: flat 10% on Qatar-source income of wholly or partly foreign-owned entities, tax on the foreign share; entities wholly owned by Qatari and GCC nationals resident in Qatar are not subject to CIT. Higher rates for oil and old special agreements. Pillar Two domestic top-up tax and IIR for fiscal years from 2025-01-01 (large multinationals, not our target customers). UNVERIFIED. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/qatar/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income (2026-11-18)
- Withholding: 5% on services, interest, royalties, technical fees, commissions paid to non-residents without a Qatar permanent establishment; dividends 0%; due by the 16th of the following month; monthly statement filed in Dhareeba; generally applies where the payee holds no valid tax card. UNVERIFIED. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/qatar/corporate/withholding-taxes (2026-11-18)
- Filing: calendar tax year unless GTA approves otherwise; return and payment due 4 months after period end; e-filing in Dhareeba since 2020-11-01; audited IFRS accounts with the return if capital over QAR 200,000, income over QAR 500,000 or head office abroad; Arabic financial statements; books kept in Qatar for 10 years. UNVERIFIED. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/qatar/corporate/tax-administration (2026-11-18)
- Other: excise tax since 2019 (tobacco, energy drinks, alcohol, pork, sweetened and carbonated drinks); no personal income tax on salaries; employer social insurance for Qatari employees only. UNVERIFIED. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/qatar/corporate/other-taxes (2026-11-18)
- Pillar Two registration opened on Dhareeba (August 2026): shows Dhareeba is where new services land. UNVERIFIED (PwC alert 403 on fetch; seen in search results). https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/services/tax/middle-east-tax-news-alerts/2026/qatar-general-tax-authority-launches-pillar-two-registration-on-the-dhareeba-platform.html

### What the platform must do (proposed)
- Customers: owner-led firms are mostly Qatari/GCC-owned (CIT-exempt, but may still file) or foreign-owned or mixed (CIT 10% on the foreign share). The platform keeps the ledger, produces IFRS-based statements and a CIT working paper; the filing itself is by the customer or accountant in Dhareeba. No known public filing API (UNVERIFIED, not searched deeply), so no integration to certify.
- Tax code table: ship with "no VAT" and an empty, versioned, effective-dated tax-rate slot so VAT can be switched on by configuration when enacted. Do not hard-code 5%.
- WHT: a rule (configuration) flags non-resident service payments, computes 5% at the configured rate, files nothing itself, outputs the monthly statement data.
- Deterministic code: CIT and WHT arithmetic and the foreign-ownership share split. Configuration: rates, thresholds, deadlines, exemption flags.
- Open risks: VAT announcement timing; ownership-share treatment; treaty rates; what counts as service payments; Qatari-owned firms' own filing duties.

## Q2 E-invoicing
- Cabinet approved a draft e-invoicing law and implementing regulations on 2026-05-06, prepared by the Ministry of Finance with the GTA. Reported scope: B2B, B2G and B2C; expected model clearance for B2B/B2G and reporting for B2C (EY expectation, not stated in the law); staggered phases with large businesses first; implementation "anticipated" from 2027-01-01 but EY says no dates are confirmed; still to pass Shura Council and the Amir. Draft text not published (KPMG). Penalties and timeline are left to the executive regulations. A pilot with large companies since late 2025 is reported by one source only. UNVERIFIED. https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/qatar-approves-draft-e-invoicing-law-and-implementing-regulations ; https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/05/qatar-cabinet-approves-draft-law-e-invoicing-executive-regulations.html ; https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/06/05/qatar-cabinet-approves-draft-e-invoicing-law-and-regulations/ (all 2026-11-18, KPMG and vatupdate read only as search excerpts)
- GTA site does not mention e-invoicing (VERIFIED, https://www.gta.gov.qa/en, 2026-11-18). Enactment status after August 2026 is unknown.
- Platform: the invoice component already issues immutable numbered documents ([e-invoicing.md](e-invoicing.md)). Plan a Qatar e-invoicing adapter slot behind a feature flag, with no format assumptions (do not assume UBL or ZATCA-style signing). Customers need no certification today. No law, no integration.
- Deterministic code: numbering, immutability, tax lines (once a tax exists). Configuration: format, clearance endpoint, phase date per taxpayer.
- Open risks: format (UBL, PEPPOL-like?), clearance vs reporting, whether an e-invoicing mandate precedes VAT, whether certified solution providers are needed, whether foreign SaaS vendors must register.

## Q3 Data protection and hosting
- Law No. 13 of 2016 (Personal Data Privacy Protection): general law, first in the GCC; covers personal data processed electronically (or mixed); consent generally required with listed exceptions; prior notice to data subjects; sensitive data (health, ethnicity, religion, relationships, criminal records) needs regulator authorisation and an impact assessment; no registration and no mandatory DPO found; controllers must appoint and train responsible staff; fines up to QAR 5 million reported. UNVERIFIED. https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?c=QA&t=law (2026-11-18)
- Regulator: NCSA (National Cyber Governance and Assurance Affairs). Earlier 2021 guidelines came from the Ministry of Transport and Communications (Compliance and Data Protection Department); NCSA issued individuals' rights guidelines (April 2026), a data sharing checklist (May 2026) and a cloud privacy assessment tool (2026). UNVERIFIED (news and law-firm summaries). https://www.thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/18/04/2026/explainer-ncsa-issues-guidelines-on-individuals-personal-data-privacy-protection-law-in-qatar ; https://s.thepeninsula.qa/article/25/05/2026/ncsa-launches-personal-data-sharing-checklist-to-reinforce-privacy-compliance (2026-11-18)
- Breach notification: notify the regulator and individuals as soon as possible, within 72 hours where damage is likely (one summary; another source says unconfirmed against the text). UNVERIFIED.
- Cross-border transfer: DLA Piper summary says transfers rely on consent or lawful purpose and the controller must show compliance; flows should not be restricted unless the processing breaks the law or causes gross damage. No general data localisation rule was described. UNVERIFIED.
- Government cloud: MCIT Cloud First Policy v1.0.0 (government agencies; data classified C0 to C4 reported, top class exempt from cloud; endorsed cloud providers; NIA and NCSA Cloud Security Policy). The PDF could not be read by the fetch tool. National Data Classification Policy (NCSA, applies to government and critical sectors, per a summary). UNVERIFIED. https://hukoomi.gov.qa/-/media/df/assets/documents/p005_cloudfirst_v100_en_0.pdf ; https://marhaba.qa/ncsa-highlights-key-pillars-of-data-classification-policy/ (2026-11-18)
- Financial sector: QCB Cloud Computing regulation issued April 2024; QCB Data Handling and Protection Regulation (reported February 2025) requires supervised institutions to keep the main customer data environment inside Qatar unless QCB approves; material cloud outsourcing needs QCB approval. Applies to supervised financial institutions, not to a general SME. UNVERIFIED. https://www.qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2024-04/21/0051-qcb-issues-cloud-computing-regulations ; https://www.gulf-times.com/article/737122/qatar/data-residency-rules-reshape-payment-technology-in-qatar-and-the-gulf (2026-11-18)
- QFC: own Data Protection Regulations and Rules 2021, GDPR-style, with a Commissioner. Applies to QFC entities. UNVERIFIED. https://www.qfc.qa/media/xp3pqif3/qfc_data_protection_regulations_and_rules_2021_guidance_-_v31.pdf (2026-11-18, seen in search results)
- Residency position (proposed, to confirm with counsel): no general in-country hosting duty found for private SMEs. Open Q-9 and Q-14 in country-matrix: a Qatar or nearby GCC region may still be needed by commercial contracts, bank or government customers and for sensitive (health) data. Treat as a per-tenant residency setting defaulting to the region chosen in the residency doc ([data-protection-and-residency.md](data-protection-and-residency.md)). Do not promise "in Qatar" hosting until a cloud region and a customer demand exist (UNVERIFIED, no source on region availability checked).
- Platform duties: consent and notice records, data subject rights flows, breach runbook with a 72h clock configured as a per-country parameter, processor contract terms for our role, sensitive-data flag (health, religion) on HR fields, tenant data export and erasure. Deterministic code: deadlines, retention, redaction. Configuration: notice text, regulator contact, residency region.
- Open risks: treatment of our company as processor vs controller; whether foreign processors need extra notice; sensitive-data authorisation for HR data (medical leave); consistency of 72h claim.

## Q4 Payroll and labour
- Wage Protection System (WPS): private-sector employers under Labour Law 14 of 2004 pay wages through Qatar-licensed banks; the employer sends a Salary Information File (SIF, a CSV with worker name, QID, IBAN and amount; banks may set their own format); salaries are paid within 7 days of the due date as reported by one guide; fines up to QAR 6,000 per breach and visa or hiring restrictions; government bodies, embassies and QFC entities generally outside. Details (field layout, legal basis, Ministry check against employment contracts) differ across sources. UNVERIFIED. https://www.dohaguides.com/wage-protection-system-wps-qatar/ ; https://www.greythr.com/middle-east/blog/qatar-wps-compliance-2026/ (2026-11-18)
- End-of-service gratuity: Labour Law gives employees with at least one year of continuous service a gratuity of not less than three weeks of basic wage per year of service (Article 54 per most sources, one source says Article 15), lost only for gross misconduct (Article 61 reported). QFC employment rules do not require gratuity. UNVERIFIED. https://www.gulf-times.com/story/371296/qatar-labour-law-end-of-service-benefits ; https://webiis08.mondaq.com/employee-rights-labour-relations/459382/the-qatar-labour-law-and-the-qfc-employment-regulations-the-key-differences-explained (2026-11-18)
- Hours: 8 hours a day and 48 a week; Ramadan 6 and 36; overtime premium at least 25% (more on rest days or holidays); managerial staff outside the weekly cap. Article numbers 73 and 74 reported. UNVERIFIED (conflicting source says 4 hours in Ramadan). https://www.dohaguides.com/qatar-labour-law-about-working-hours/ ; https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/guides/qatar-labour-law-employers-need-know (2026-11-18)
- Tax and social: no employee income tax; employer social insurance (pension) only for Qatari nationals. UNVERIFIED (PwC link above under Q1).
- Platform for 10-100 staff firms: payroll items = basic wage, allowances, overtime, deductions, leave, gratuity accrual, WPS SIF export per bank, Qatari pension (rates and caps not researched). Deterministic code: gratuity, overtime and proration formulas and SIF generation. Configuration: bank file layout, rates, Ramadan hours, holiday set. Payroll is out of scope of AI authorship (AGENTS.md).
- Integration or certification: no vendor certification found; bank-specific SIF templates need testing with at least one bank. National workforce quotas (Qatarisation) not researched.
- Open risks: SIF layout per bank; exact WPS timing and variable-pay split; gratuity base (basic only vs including allowances) and part-year rule; Qatari pension rates; domestic worker and QFC/free-zone employees under different rules; 2024 or later Labour Law amendments unchecked.

## Q5 Accounting standard
- IFRS: listed companies must use IFRS (QFMA rules, per IFAC profile); the tax administration page says accounts filed with the return must follow IFRS; Commercial Companies Law 11 of 2015 as amended by Law 8 of 2021 and the audit profession Law 8 of 2020 are cited by the Ministry of Commerce auditor guide. Audit scope for LLCs is disputed between sources (a QAR 5 million income threshold in one, broad LLC duty in another). Arabic statutory statements; books kept in Qatar for 10 years (see Q1). UNVERIFIED. https://www.ifac.org/about-ifac/membership/profile/qatar ; https://www.qatarliving.com/en/article/audited-financial-statements-qatar-requirements-deadlines ; https://moci.gov.qa/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/EN-دليل-خدمات-مدققي-الحسابات.pdf (2026-11-18)
- No mandatory national chart of accounts found (not searched deeply). Platform: IFRS-based default chart (ties to Q-8), Arabic and English account names, ledger exportable for auditors, Arabic statement output, 10-year retention in a region compatible with the Qatar-books requirement (open question for counsel). IFRS for SMEs use vs full IFRS is undecided. Deterministic code: posting, closing, statements. Configuration: chart template and statement layouts.
- Open risks: IFRS for SMEs permitted or not; audit threshold; whether the "books kept in Qatar" rule allows cloud copies abroad.

## Q6 Calendars
- Week: Friday rest day normal under the Labour Law (24 consecutive hours, article 75 reported); Saturday is commonly also off but varies by employer; sources give no firm statement. UNVERIFIED. https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/guides/qatar-labour-law-employers-need-know (2026-11-18)
- Holidays: Ministry of Labour announces private-sector holidays: Eid al-Fitr (3 days in 2026), Eid al-Adha (3 days), National Day 18 December; National Sports Day (second Tuesday of February) is also a paid public holiday per one source. Islamic holidays depend on moon sighting and an announcement, so dates cannot be computed in advance. UNVERIFIED. https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/15/03/2026/labour-ministry-announces-three-day-eid-al-fitr-holiday-for-private-sector ; https://s.thepeninsula.qa/article/24/05/2026/qatar-declares-three-day-eid-al-adha-holiday-for-private-sector ; https://s.thepeninsula.qa/article/16/12/2025/labour-ministry-announces-national-day-holiday-for-private-sector (2026-11-18)
- Platform: Gregorian civil and fiscal calendar; Hijri used for Islamic holidays (see [../architecture/calendars-and-locale.md](../architecture/calendars-and-locale.md)); holidays are a per-tenant editable list that an announcement overrides, never computed silently; Ramadan hours toggle; fiscal year defaults to the calendar year (GTA can approve another).
- Open risks: tabular Hijri vs announced dates; number of paid holidays under the Labour Law; Ramadan hours (6 vs 4).

## Q7 Company and licensing
- Law No. 1 of 2019 allows 100% foreign ownership in most sectors; excluded areas include banking, insurance, natural resources, commercial agencies and others set by the Council of Ministers (Invest Qatar). Mainland registration through the Ministry of Commerce and Industry. A January 2026 report says Cabinet approved reforms to the foreign investment law; content not read. UNVERIFIED. https://www.invest.qa/en/resources/laws-and-regulations/foreign-ownership ; https://www.lexis.ae/2026/01/15/qtr-cabinet-approves-sweeping-reforms-to-foreign-investment-law/ (2026-11-18)
- Free zones and financial centre: QFC (own law, regulations, tax and data rules), QSTP and Qatar Free Zones (Ras Bufontas, Umm Al Houl) offer 100% foreign ownership; entities there fall under separate regimes. QFC and QSTP specifics not verified. UNVERIFIED. https://www.gulf-times.com/article/724861/qatar/qatars-quiet-investment-boom-14500-foreign-firms-and-counting
- For us as a vendor: serving Qatari customers as a SaaS likely needs no Qatar entity to sell but may need one for local contracts, invoicing and VAT later; decision belongs to LEG-010 and counsel (LEG-003). Customers' own entity types (LLC, branch, sole establishment, free zone) change payroll (WPS exemption in QFC), audit and data rules, so the Blueprint must record entity regime as a field. UNVERIFIED.
- Open risks: whether our selling to Qatar customers triggers WHT on us (customers withholding 5% on service fees to a non-resident without a tax card), commercial agency rules, data-hosting contract terms.
