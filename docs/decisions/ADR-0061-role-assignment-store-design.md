## Decision

### 1. Shape and placement
1. **One store per served country**, in that country, with backups, replicas, logs and keys in the same country (ADR-0060 item 14; hosting-rules lines 11, 15, 16). Countries are never replicated, joined or queried together. A market with no in-country location for both the store and its anchor (section 4) is not served; Egypt and Qatar today (UNVERIFIED, pending INF-001, a founder decision and counsel).
2. **Its own logical database**, not the control-plane workflow database, in its own network segment, reachable only from the assignment service. `S22` and the provisioning workflow reach nothing in it.
3. **Technology.** A relational database with row-locking transactions, row-level security that can be forced on table owners, transaction-local session settings, and stored procedures. PostgreSQL is the reference shape for review (`FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, `set_config(..., true)`); its behaviour on a pinned version, its licence and hosting are UNVERIFIED until INF-001 and LEG-004.
4. **The assignment service** in `services/control-plane/` (Eldon's lane) has three separately deployed parts with separate credentials and keys: the **read path**, the **write service** (the only writer, ADR-0060 item 6) and the **verifier** (drift and anchor checks, section 7). The identity service, which signs the assertions in section 5, is a separate deployment with its own signing key; no assignment-service part holds an identity key. The gateway (`services/gateway/`) and the adapter (`apps/engine/`) are partner's lane and consume interfaces fixed by later contract ADRs.

### 2. Schema (logical)
Every table is keyed by the platform-generated `tenantId` (ADR-0040 line 16). Members are identified by `memberRef`, the identity service's per-tenant membership id: random, never reused, never a person id, email or phone (ADR-0060 item 14). The store holds no names. The `RoleResolver` adapter maps the verified person to `memberRef` through the membership the token names.

| Table | Key | Columns |
|---|---|---|
| `tenant_state` | `tenantId` | `status` (`active`, `apply_fenced`, `restore_fenced`, `site_fenced`, `deleting`; section 3.1), `generation`, `lastIssuedVersion`, `lastSeq`, `stateCounter` (raised by one on every change to the row, fences included; D-3), `lastVerifiedSyncVersion`, `appliedRevision` and digest, `catalogDigest` (2.6), `memberRoot` (2.7), `historyHead`, `bootstrapped`, `stateSig` |
| `fence_notice` | `tenantId`, `seq` | Fences raised by the verifier (N-L1): reason, time, `fenceSig` under the verifier's fence-only key. Append-only. Any unlifted notice makes reads deny |
| `tenant_tombstone` | `tenantId` | Permanent; written at tenant deletion only after the anchored `tenant_delete` entry (5.6); never deleted |
| `role_catalog` | `tenantId`, `roleId` | From the applied revision: `function`, `isAdmin`, scope per granted entity, `revisionDigest`. Bound to the signed state and the anchor by `catalogDigest` (2.6); never trusted on its own |
| `assignment` | `tenantId`, `memberRef` | `granted` and `usable` (encrypted role sets with scopes), `setAtVersion`, `cipherGeneration` (the generation the ciphertexts were made under), `lastSeq` (the history entry that last wrote this row), `rowSig` |
| `history_member` | `tenantId`, `memberRef`, `seq` | Append-only index: every history entry that wrote this member's row, with the row digest it produced. An audit and verifier aid only; no read decision rests on it (P-H2, 2.7) |
| `snapshot_issue` | `tenantId`, `generation`, `syncVersion` | One row per issued snapshot, first writer wins (unique key): canonical payload hash, `identityViewHash` (4.5), `issueSig` under the snapshot key; written by the read path through one insert-if-absent procedure. A retry at the same version must reproduce this hash (P-M2) |
| `history` | `tenantId`, `seq` | One entry per accepted event, `seq` gap-free per tenant; `syncVersion` set only on entries that change `granted` (`bootstrap`, `change`, `member_removed`, `catalog_refresh`, `catalog_revert`, `restore_reconciled`): `kind` (`bootstrap`, `change`, `member_removed`, `catalog_refresh`, `catalog_revert`, `apply_verified`, `sync_verified`, `status`, `restore_reconciled`, `tenant_delete`), `generation`, `memberRef`, encrypted before and after sets, `approvalRequestId` (unique per tenant), the signed assertions or evidence that authorised it, per member written the **row digest** (hash of the resulting `granted` and `usable` ciphertexts, `setAtVersion`, `cipherGeneration`), the resulting `catalogDigest` and `memberRoot`, `prevHash`, `entryHash`, commit time. Append-only. A `resync` kind allocates a fresh version with unchanged content, for leaving `site_fenced` |
| `refusal_log` | `tenantId`, `seq` | Local outbox of refusal records (2.8) and fence entries (3.1), never dropped. Append-only, own hash chain, not anchored, forwarded to the SEC-008 `AuditStore`, which is the record of reference |

1. **History is the store's audit trail** (ADR-0060 item 12), tenant-visible through a per-tenant audit view. `entryHash` covers the entry header (tenant, `seq`, version, generation, kind, `memberRef`, request id, assertion hashes, row digests, `catalogDigest`, `memberRoot`) and the ciphertexts plus `prevHash`, so the chain still verifies after the tenant's key is destroyed (Q-582).
2. **Encryption happens in the service, not in the database** (R-M1). Role sets are encrypted with authenticated encryption under a per-tenant **assignment key**, a separate key from `S10b` (R-M5) wrapped by an in-country KMS key used only by the assignment service, and destroyed together with `S10b` at crypto-shred (ADR-0040 line 49). Associated data: `tenantId`, `memberRef`, column name (`granted`, `usable`, `before`, `after`), `setAtVersion` and the generation it was made under (`cipherGeneration` on the row). A ciphertext moved to another row, column or version fails to decrypt, and a failed decrypt denies. Because of this binding no check ever compares a row ciphertext with a history ciphertext: integrity between them goes through the row digest recorded in history (N-M1). This stops misplaced or swapped ciphertext. It does not protect against a compromised read path, which can unwrap every tenant's assignment key in the country. Unwrapped keys stay in process memory only, for a bounded time set in the runbook (proposed 5 minutes); KMS unreachable with no unexpired key denies.
3. **Signed rows.** The write service signs every `assignment` row (`rowSig`: tenant, member, both ciphertext hashes, `setAtVersion`, `cipherGeneration`, `lastSeq`) and the `tenant_state` row (`stateSig`: status, generation, versions, `stateCounter`, head) with a per-country **store-state key** in KMS, non-exportable, held only by the write service. The read path verifies with the public key. A direct edit that swaps `granted` into `usable`, lifts a fence or changes a version fails verification and denies (R-M4). A signature proves the bytes were once signed, not that they are current: replay of an older signed state is handled in 6.2 (N-M4).
4. **Rebuild at any version** by folding `history`; used by the verifier and reconciliation, never by a live read.
5. **No other data.** No display names, contacts, agent grants or support principals (ADR-0060 item 15).
6. **Catalog bound to the approved revision** (P-H1). `catalogDigest` is the hash of the tenant's canonical catalog (every `role_catalog` row, sorted by `roleId`: `function`, `isAdmin`, scopes) together with the approved revision digest it was derived from. It is written only by `catalog_refresh` or `catalog_revert` (5.5), recorded in that anchored history entry and covered by `stateSig`. The catalog rows are derived from the Blueprint registry entry whose content hash equals the approved revision digest; nothing else writes them. A flag or scope edited in place therefore no longer matches `catalogDigest`.
7. **Member commitment** (P-H2). `memberRoot` is the hash over the sorted list of (`memberRef`, `lastSeq`, row digest) of every `assignment` row of the tenant. Every write recomputes it in the same transaction, records it in the anchored history entry and covers it with `stateSig`. A member with no row has no leaf. Tenants have 10 to 100 staff, so the read path recomputes it from the tenant's row headers on each read (one indexed range scan); a Merkle inclusion proof over the same leaves is an allowed implementation of the same check if a tenant cap in the runbook is exceeded.
8. **Refusal records** (P-M4; ADR-0060 item 12). Every request that reaches the write service and is refused produces one record, before the refusal is returned: reason code; actor `memberRef` and `token_id` and approver `memberRef` where known; target `memberRef`; roles and scopes before and after from the request; payload hash; the tenant's current and the expected `syncVersion`; the request's one audit id, carried end to end from the gateway (ADR-0069 2.12); time. Records go to the `refusal_log` outbox and on to the SEC-008 `AuditStore`, which owns storage, tenant visibility and deduplication (deduplication on the audit id in the `AuditStore` adapter only, not here). A refusal with no verified tenant (failed token, tenant mismatch, forged evidence naming another tenant) goes to the separate platform security stream, not to any tenant's view. Nothing is dropped: the rate limit (5.3) refuses before the tenant lock but after the record, and floods are stopped earlier by the gateway's per-caller limits, where each limited request is itself recorded by the gateway's audit (ADR-0069) under the same audit id. If the record cannot be written, the request is still refused and the write service stops accepting writes for that tenant until the outbox is writable again (fail closed; reads are unaffected).

### 3. What the database enforces and what the service enforces (R-M1)
- **Database (procedures, constraints, grants):** version allocation in the write transaction; gap-free history with `prevHash` linking; unique `approvalRequestId`; compare-and-set on the target row's `setAtVersion`; only the writes permitted for the tenant's status (3.1); refusal of bootstrap against a tombstone, a set `bootstrapped` flag or an existing `bootstrap` or `change` entry; `member_removed` and `tenant_delete` can only delete rows, and the row deletes of `tenant_delete` only after its anchored entry is committed; `snapshot_issue` first writer wins per (`generation`, `syncVersion`); promotion only for a receipt whose generation equals `tenant_state.generation` and whose payload hash equals the `snapshot_issue` hash for its version; `usable` promotion only up to the receipt version (6.3); every row write must carry the row digest recorded in the history entry appended in the same transaction, and `history_member` is appended with it. Row-level security is forced on every table, so procedure owners are bound too (UNVERIFIED on the pinned version), and procedures also take the tenant as an argument and set it transaction-local.
- **Write service:** verification of the signed assertions (section 5), the evaluator veto (5.3), the catalog digest check (5.3) and role catalog and `scope_conflict` checks, the member commitment, last-Owner rule, narrowing and widening classification, encryption and re-encryption (including for promotion and reconciliation, which the database cannot do because it never sees plaintext or the signing key), row and state signatures, anchor append. The verifier re-checks promotion and reconciliation results against the anchor; a reconciled tenant stays fenced until it has (N-M1).
- **Plainly:** a compromised write service can grant any role in its country, because the authorisation checks run there. It cannot reuse a version, skip the anchor or replay an approval without the anchor or the database refusing, and every grant it forges is detected by the verifier, which re-verifies every history entry's assertions with identity's public key (section 7). Compromise of both the write service and identity's assertion key grants roles undetected by signature; that residual stays (T53).

#### 3.1 Permitted writes per status (N-M2)
Entering any fenced status is a signed `tenant_state` update (raising `stateCounter`) plus a `refusal_log` fence entry; it never needs the anchor, so it works when the anchor is down or ahead. When the anchor is reachable the write service also appends a non-blocking fence marker (tenant, `stateCounter`, status) to it, so the read path's anchor-head refresh sees the fence (D-3). Leaving a fenced status is an anchored history entry (`status`). Fencing only removes access, so any assignment-service part or the apply orchestration may request it; lifting needs the evidence below. If a fence cannot be written at all, the write service stops serving writes and the verifier runs at once; reads deny anyway while the database is unreachable.

| Status | Set by | Writes permitted while in it | Lifted by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `active` | lifting any fence | all | n/a |
| `apply_fenced` | the apply orchestration of ADR-0039 item 8, through the write service, naming the revision digest | `catalog_refresh` (on an interim receipt), `catalog_revert`, `bootstrap` (provisioning only), sync receipts and promotion, `member_removed`, fences | the adapter's signed **final** apply receipt for that digest, given after the full sync and drift check at a version at or after the refresh (5.5); an interim receipt never lifts it; anchored |
| `site_fenced` | the write service, or a verifier `fence_notice` for engine drift or a site version or generation below the store's (4.8) | `resync` (fresh version), sync receipts, `member_removed`, fences | a verified full sync at the `resync` version and a passing drift check; anchored |
| `restore_fenced` | restore detection (4.4, 4.6), the restore procedure, a verifier `fence_notice` for a chain or anchor mismatch | reconciliation entries only (4.7) | `restore_reconciled` after the verifier re-checked the result against the anchor; anchored |
| `deleting` | the anchored `tenant_delete` entry (5.6); unlike the fences, entering it needs the anchor | `tenant_delete` steps only | never; ends in the tombstone |

**Fence notices (D-4).** The write service maps each verifier `fence_notice` to a status by its reason: chain, checkpoint or anchor mismatch, or a forged history entry, to `restore_fenced`; engine drift, site version or generation to `site_fenced`; any other reason to `restore_fenced`. A row that no longer matches live memberships fences nothing (identity already denies that member); it starts the `member_removed` path instead. A notice counts as lifted only when an anchored `status` entry names its id; the write service writes that entry together with the lift of the mapped status, under that status's evidence. Until then reads deny, whatever `tenant_state.status` says.

### 4. Versions, anchor and snapshots
1. **Anchor.** An append-only log per country, in-country, in a separate cloud account or project from the store, with its own credentials: the write service holds append-and-read only; the verifier and the audit reader hold read only; the read path holds a read-only credential for per-tenant heads and fence markers only, never entries (D-2); no principal holds update or delete, enforced by the storage itself (object lock or equivalent; UNVERIFIED per provider). It holds, per tenant, every history entry as written (header, ciphertexts, hashes, assertions) and a signed checkpoint per entry under the store-state key. It is a full encrypted copy of the assignment history, so it is placed and protected like the store (ADR-0060 items 7, 12, 14). Whether every market has such a home is UNVERIFIED (R-L6).
2. **Conditional append.** The anchor storage enforces create-if-absent on the key (`tenantId`, `seq`), so one entry, and so one version, exists per `seq` (UNVERIFIED per provider). The write service only appends `seq = lastSeq + 1`; the `prevHash` link is checked by the verifier, not by the storage (N-L3).
3. **Anchor before effect** (R-H2). A write runs in one database transaction that locks the tenant row, allocates the next `seq` and, for a `granted` change, `v = lastIssuedVersion + 1`, writes history and rows, then appends that entry to the anchor and commits only after the anchor acknowledges. Nothing is usable, no snapshot is signed and no caller is told "done" before the commit. If the anchor is unreachable or refuses, the transaction rolls back and the write is refused; this blocks narrowing changes too during an anchor outage, which is accepted, because revocation goes through identity (6.6). The anchor call has a fixed timeout set in the runbook.
4. **Failure between the two.** Anchor acknowledged, database commit failed: the anchor is ahead. Anchor appended without acknowledgement, database rolled back: the next write's append for that `seq` is refused as a duplicate. Either way the tenant moves to `restore_fenced` (reads deny; entering the fence needs no anchor, 3.1) and section 4.7 applies. No version is issued twice with different content, because the anchor holds the only accepted entry for each version. When the acknowledgement is lost the caller is told "outcome unknown", not "failed": reconciliation may then apply the change, which is intended, because the anchor is the commit point. A lost acknowledgement fences the tenant until two people reconcile; that is an availability cost and a way for anyone who can disturb the anchor link to fence tenants (N-L3, section 10).
5. **Snapshot binding.** The read path signs each full snapshot for `sync_members` with a per-country **snapshot key** (KMS, non-exportable, assignment service only), over `tenantId`, `generation`, `syncVersion`, the canonical payload hash, `issuedAt` and a short validity (proposed 10 minutes, R-L4); the adapter's clock comes from the platform's in-country time source with a tolerance of 60 seconds (proposed; UNVERIFIED until INF-001), beyond which it refuses (N-L6). The adapter refuses a snapshot whose signature fails, whose tenant is not the tenant its store is bound to (ADR-0041), whose `generation` is lower than the highest it has accepted, whose version is not above its last applied one (ADP-C-063), or that has expired; nothing changes. A signature rather than a shared MAC, so the adapter can verify but not mint. `displayName` (`sync-members.schema.json` line 26) is joined from identity when the snapshot is built, covered by the payload hash, never stored here (Q-580). **Identity data is pinned per version** (P-M2): the builder asks identity for a signed identity view (tenant, the `memberRef` list, the names, a view hash), identity keeps that view by its hash for the snapshot's life (Q-663), and the first build for a (`generation`, `syncVersion`) records the payload hash and `identityViewHash` in `snapshot_issue`. A retry at the same version rebuilds from the same pinned view and must produce the recorded payload hash; if the view is gone or the hash differs, the retry is refused and a fresh version is issued with `resync`. No two payloads are ever signed for one version.
6. **Detecting a store restore.** On start, after any restore, and on every verifier run, each tenant's `lastIssuedVersion` and `historyHead` are compared with the anchor. Store behind or different: `restore_fenced`. A restore procedure also fences every tenant of that store before the service starts. An anchored `tenant_delete` for the tenant wins over everything (P-M3): the tenant moves to `deleting`, the deletion steps of 5.6 are re-applied (tombstone written, rows deleted, key destruction confirmed) and it is never reconciled or reactivated.
7. **Reconciliation is a deterministic replay, never a free-form rebuild** (R-H3). The reconciliation tool (reviewed code in the assignment service) reads the tenant's anchored chain, verifies every link and checkpoint signature, and replays it to compute the assignment rows. **Each event's evidence is re-validated on its own** (P-H3): before an entry is applied in the replay, the tool rebuilds the canonical payload from the entry itself (tenant, `memberRef`, the decrypted before and after sets, `expectedSetAtVersion`, request id) and checks the recorded evidence against it with public keys only, using the replayed state as of the previous entry: for `change`, both identity assertions over that payload's action-policy hash, kinds, step-up, expiry against the anchored commit time, SOD-DIFF and SOD-NOT-SELF, the initiator's `function: owner` and the approver's owner or admin role in the replayed `usable` sets and replayed catalog, `rolesBefore` and `expectedSetAtVersion` against the replayed row, catalog membership and `scope_conflict`, the last-Owner rule; for `bootstrap`, the identity statement and the one-owner-role rule; for `catalog_refresh` and `catalog_revert`, the approvals and receipts of 5.5 and a catalog re-derived from the registry by the approved digest; for `member_removed` and `tenant_delete`, the signed statements of 5.6; for `sync_verified` and `apply_verified`, the adapter receipt against the recorded `snapshot_issue` hash; for `status`, the evidence named in 3.1; for `restore_reconciled`, that its rows equal the replay at that point. Live checks that cannot be repeated later (the evaluator veto, current session versions) are not re-asked: the evaluator can only veto, and the signed evidence is what grants. One entry that fails stops the replay with no result, not a partial one: the tenant stays `restore_fenced`, it is a security incident with tenant notification, and the recovery path is Q-661. The verifier runs the same checks in its own code path in its own deployment, not by calling the tool. Because reconciliation increments `generation`, the tool decrypts the replayed sets and re-encrypts and re-signs them under the new generation (N-M1). The database procedure accepts the result only with a `restore_reconciled` entry that records the anchored head it replayed to and the new row digests, continues `seq` from the anchor, and refuses anything else. Before the fence lifts, the verifier independently replays the anchor, decrypts the new rows and compares; any difference keeps the tenant fenced. Two people approve or refuse the result; they never edit it. A chain that does not verify leaves the tenant fenced, and the tenant is notified. The tool then records `restore_reconciled`, increments `generation`, and sets `lastIssuedVersion` above the maximum of the anchor's, the store's and the adapter's last applied version. `usable` for each member becomes the narrower of the restored and the replayed set until a full sync at the new generation verifies (6.3). Rows of members whose membership identity reports as removed are then removed (5.6). With two founders the two approvers are the founders (T52), and staff abroad acting on in-country data is a Q-14 question.
8. **Site restore.** `lastVerifiedSyncVersion` comes only from verified receipts (6.3). A site reporting a lower version or generation is restored or tampered: `site_fenced` until a full sync at a fresh `resync` version and the full drift check pass (ADR-0060 item 8; ADR-0039 item 9, line 49).

### 5. Writers and their authorisation
1. **Database roles**, each its own secret (ids assigned when SEC-002 updates secrets-management.md; none is `S22`):
   - `assign_reader`: `SELECT` on `assignment`, `tenant_state`, `role_catalog`, `fence_notice`, `history_member`, `snapshot_issue` and the row digest of history entries, and `EXECUTE` on the insert-if-absent `snapshot_issue` procedure only (it can record a version's first payload hash, never change one), under forced row-level security; read path only.
   - `assign_writer`: `EXECUTE` on the write procedures only, no direct DML; write service only.
   - `assign_bootstrap`: `EXECUTE` on the bootstrap procedure only; the write service's bootstrap handler, never provisioning.
   - `assign_audit_reader`: `SELECT` on `history`, `history_member`, `refusal_log`, `tenant_state`, `assignment`, `fence_notice`; the verifier, reconciliation and the tenant audit view (filtered by tenant) (R-L2, N-L1).
   - `assign_verifier`: `INSERT` on `fence_notice` only, with a fence-only signing key the read path accepts; the verifier can fence and nothing else. The verifier also holds unwrap rights on the assignment keys to compare decrypted sets, a third holder of every tenant's key in the country (section 10). If it cannot write a notice it pages on-call and the runbook stops the write service (N-L1).
   - Schema owner: the reviewed migration pipeline only (IaC, hosting-rules line 13); a migration that changes assignment data is refused in review; credential sealed, two-person, alert on use.
2. **Approval assertions** (R-H1). Each human decision on an `AP-PERM-01` request produces an **assertion signed by identity** at the moment the person acts: kind (`initiate` or `approve`), `tenantId`, the actor's `memberRef`, principal kind `human`, session version, `approvalRequestId`, the canonical payload hash, the time of a step-up within 300 seconds (ADR-0007 item 6, line 23), issue time and a one-hour expiry. The payload hash is the single action-policy payload hash (action-policy-table line 64), whose canonical payload includes the five fields of 5.3; the approver's screen is rendered from that same canonical payload (N-L2). Identity signs only for a verified human session in that tenant with a fresh step-up; it never signs for agent, assistant, model, staff or service principals in the customer realm. Staff-realm assertions (signed by identity's staff realm for a verified staff human with step-up) are accepted only as the staff approval of a `catalog_refresh` (5.5) and the second approval of a `tenant_delete` (5.6), never for `AP-PERM-01` or any other write (D-6). The assertion key is identity's own, in-country KMS, separate from its token-signing key. The action-policy evaluator keeps its request records in the control plane in-country, outside the tenant site; those records drive the workflow and the screens but are **not** sufficient for authorisation: they carry the assertions as evidence, and the evaluator's state is a veto (5.3), never a grant.
3. **A change is accepted only if all hold at execution time:**
   - the payload contains `{tenantId, memberRef, rolesBefore, rolesAfter, expectedSetAtVersion}` (`expectedSetAtVersion` is 0 and `rolesBefore` empty for a member with no row) and the two assertions are over its action-policy hash; both signatures verify;
   - the evaluator answers, at execution, that the request is `approved`: not cancelled, voided or expired, and under the same table version (action-policy-table lines 73-79); no answer or any other answer denies. The evaluator can veto, never mint (N-M3);
   - the initiator assertion's actor holds a role with `function: owner` and the approver's a role with `function: owner` or `isAdmin: true`, both read from their current `usable` sets and the catalog flags, not from role names (R-L5); **before** that check the write service reads the whole catalog, recomputes `catalogDigest` and requires it to equal the verified `stateSig` value and the digest in the latest anchored `catalog_refresh` or `catalog_revert` entry, and the catalog's `revisionDigest` to equal `appliedRevision`; any mismatch denies and fences the tenant `restore_fenced` (P-H1). Bootstrap (5.4) and catalog checks in 5.5 use the same verified catalog. The verifier independently re-derives the catalog from the Blueprint registry by the approved revision digest and compares it with the rows, `catalogDigest` and the anchor (7.2); both memberships are active and their session versions still current (asked of identity; no answer denies);
   - approver differs from initiator; neither is the target (SOD-DIFF, SOD-NOT-SELF; Q-41);
   - the assertions are unexpired; `approvalRequestId` has not been used (database unique constraint) (R-M3);
   - the target row's `setAtVersion` equals `expectedSetAtVersion` and its decrypted `granted` equals `rolesBefore` (R-M3);
   - the tenant equals the tenant of the verified context that submits the execution; who submits does not matter, because the authority is the two assertions;
   - every role id is in `role_catalog`, the result has no `scope_conflict`, MFA is enrolled for the target where a role needs it;
   - the change does not leave the tenant with no active member holding a `function: owner` role (last-Owner rule, R-M8). The rule applies to `change` only (N-M6).
   A refusal is recorded in full (2.8), changes nothing, and is rate-limited per member and per tenant before any lock is taken (R-L3); a rate-limited request is recorded too.
4. **Bootstrap** (the one exception ADR-0060 item 6 names; R-M2). During ADR-0040 Step 7, after the initial revision is applied and its catalog recorded (5.5), the bootstrap handler writes the first Owner assignment. It accepts only a statement signed by identity binding `tenantId`, `memberRef`, the verified signup identity and the provisioning `workflowId`. The procedure refuses when a tombstone exists for the tenant, `bootstrapped` is true, or any `bootstrap` or `change` entry exists, and sets `bootstrapped` in the same transaction; the write service also refuses when the anchor holds a `tenant_delete` for the tenant (P-M3). Bootstrap runs on the catalog recorded from the interim receipt (5.5); the Owner's widening becomes usable only through the final receipt. The role must be the catalog's only role with `function: owner`; zero or several refuse.
5. **Catalog refresh** (a write outside `AP-PERM-01`; R-M7, N-M5). When a revision is applied, the write service records `catalog_refresh` only with evidence it can verify by public key: identity-signed `approve` assertions over the revision digest from the humans `AP-BPL-01` requires (the tenant's Owner, and for first clients the staff review gate, signed in identity's staff realm; for the first refresh, which runs before bootstrap gives anyone a role, "the Owner" is the same verified signup identity the bootstrap statement binds, D-6), the same pattern as 5.2, and the adapter's signed **interim receipt** for that digest and tenant. **Two receipts** (P-M1; ADR-0039 item 8, line 48, needs the membership snapshot before the adapter can report `applied`): (1) the interim receipt says the role layout for the revision is staged; it binds tenant, generation, revision digest, the apply id and the staged layout hash, is signed with the adapter's receipt key under a distinct receipt type, and is enough only to record `catalog_refresh` (and so to issue the version the sync needs, and, on first provisioning, to run bootstrap); it never lifts `apply_fenced` and never promotes `usable`. (2) the **final receipt** is given after `sync_members` at a version at or after the refresh and the full drift fingerprint pass; it binds tenant, generation, revision digest, apply id, `syncVersion`, the `snapshot_issue` payload hash and the fingerprint hash. The write service records it as `apply_verified`, promotes as in 6.3 and lifts the fence. If the adapter compensates back to the earlier layout instead, it signs a compensation receipt naming both digests, and the write service records `catalog_revert` back to the earlier, already approved revision (a restoration of prior approved state, ADR-0039 item 9; Q-662); holders' rows are rewritten as for a refresh and stay narrow. If neither arrives, or the adapter reports `recovery_required`, the tenant stays `apply_fenced`. The build state machine's evidence is not used: [ADR-0050](ADR-0050-build-state-machine.md) (Proposed) seals it with an HMAC (line 15), which a verifier could also forge, so the build machine is not a trusted party for scope changes. Roles come from the Blueprint registry by that digest, never from a request. A refresh is refused if it drops a role still assigned (`subject_in_use`, ADR-0039 line 47) or creates a `scope_conflict` in any existing assignment. A refresh that changes a role's scope rewrites every holder's row and `setAtVersion`, which voids their pending approvals by compare-and-set; this fails closed (N-L4). A scope widening is not usable until the sync verifies: each holder's `usable` keeps the narrower scope until promotion (6.3). While ADR-0039 item 8's reconciliation fence is held, the status is `apply_fenced` (3.1) and business reads deny.
6. **Remove-only lifecycle writes** (R-M8). `member_removed`: only on an identity-signed statement that the membership is removed and its session version bumped; deletes the row; **never refused by the store**, including for the last Owner (N-M6). Removal in identity always works, also while the store is down (ADR-0060 item 9). When the store answers, identity first asks whether the member is the last Owner and, if so, refuses the removal and offers suspension. When the store does not answer, the removal proceeds; if it turns out to have removed the last Owner, the tenant is ownerless and follows Q-583 (D-1). Suspension is never blocked by the last-Owner rule, so a compromised sole Owner can always be stopped. A tenant left with no Owner (for example after the person is deleted, ADR-0007 item 13) has no recovery path today: added to Q-583. `tenant_delete`: only with signed evidence of both approvals of ADR-0040 line 42 (the owner's identity-signed deletion assertion and a staff-realm second approval), never on the workflow's word. **Anchor first** (P-M3): the write service appends an anchored `tenant_delete` history entry (the approvals, the final head, time) and sets `deleting` in the same transaction, under anchor-before-effect (4.3); only after that commit does it write the tombstone and delete rows, and only then is the assignment key destroyed. An anchor outage blocks deletion, which waits; it never deletes first.
7. **Founders must confirm this reading of item 6** (Q-581, blocker): two remove-only operations (5.6), one constrained full rebuild (4.7) and catalog refresh (5.5), beside bootstrap.
8. **Staff and break-glass.** No staff, support or break-glass path writes assignments (ADR-0060 item 13); support access is an expiring PLT-019 grant, never a row here. Reconciliation is the one human-approved operation and its result is fixed by the anchor. Any other change to store data fails a signature or the chain and is a security incident with tenant notification.

### 6. Read path
1. **Every gateway request and every approved-action execution reads the store** (ADR-0060 item 10). Tenant from the verified token, `memberRef` from the membership the token names. The read path sets the tenant transaction-local before each query; a policy with the setting unset returns no rows; pooled connections never carry a tenant between transactions (R-M5).
2. **Checks on every read.** The read path itself denies (R-L1) unless: `stateSig` and `rowSig` verify; `status` is `active` and no unlifted `fence_notice` exists; the row's (`memberRef`, `lastSeq`, row digest) and every other row header of the tenant recompute to the `memberRoot` in the verified `stateSig` (2.7), and the row's digest equals its recomputed digest (stops a one-row rollback, including a stale row put back with a forged `history_member` entry while the tenant state is current, and a removed member's row put back; R-M4, N-M1, P-H2); decryption succeeds; the decrypted `usable` is a subset of `granted`; and the state is not older than this process's mark. **Replay of an older signed state** (old `tenant_state`, truncated history and old rows put back together, N-M4) is caught at read time this way: each read-path process keeps per tenant a mark, the highest (`generation`, `lastSeq`, `stateCounter`) it has verified, compared in that order, and refuses and alerts on anything lower. Database states only move forward, so normal reads never trip it. The process also raises the mark from the anchor at a fixed interval (proposed 30 seconds), but only to entries and fence markers anchored more than a grace period ago (the anchor call timeout plus a commit margin, set in the runbook): an append whose database commit is still in flight is never mistaken for a replay (D-5). Including `stateCounter` means a signed state from before a fence cannot lift it in any process that has seen the fence, directly or through the anchor marker (D-3). The anchor-head refresh also carries the head's `memberRoot`, so a whole older state that is internally consistent fails against the anchor. A replay is therefore denied at once by any process that has seen newer state, and by every process within one refresh interval; the verifier stays the backstop. The window for a freshly started process up to its first refresh is residual (section 10). Answer: `{tenantId, memberRef, generation, syncVersion, status, roles (usable)}`; the gateway checks the tenant against its claim.
3. **Narrowing now, widening after a verified sync** (ADR-0060 item 11). A removal sets `usable` to the narrower set in the same write. An addition leaves `usable` unchanged. The adapter's receipt must be signed and bound to tenant, generation, version and payload hash (contract ADR); the write service verifies it and refuses it unless its generation equals the current `tenant_state.generation` and its payload hash equals the `snapshot_issue` hash recorded for that version (P-M2), raises `lastVerifiedSyncVersion`, and after the drift fingerprint passes the write service re-encrypts the promoted sets under the `usable` column and re-signs the rows, and the promotion procedure accepts them only for rows with `setAtVersion <= ` the receipt's version, recording the new row digests in a `sync_verified` entry (R-M6, N-M1). A late receipt for version 5 never promotes a row set at version 6.
4. **Deny on every failure** (ADR-0060 item 9): unreachable, timed out, partial, malformed, wrong tenant, failed signature or decrypt, KMS unreachable, tenant not `active`, unknown member, empty role set. No fallback to the engine copy, a cached set or a default role, and no switch that enables one.
5. **No cache.** Maximum age 0 until an ADR sets one (ADR-0039 D-2, `role-and-permission-mapping.md` line 165). Primary or synchronously committing replica only; within one request memoisation is allowed, across requests it is a cache.
6. **Revocation does not depend on the store.** Identity checks the per-membership session version on every call first (ADR-0007 item 10, line 27); removal and suspension work while the store or anchor is down. Row deletion follows (5.6). No role change is possible during a store, anchor or KMS outage: accepted.
7. **Availability.** The store and the anchor have uptime targets, monitoring and runbooks before a live tenant. An outage locks out a whole country: correct, and a denial-of-service target.

### 7. Sync, drift and the verifier
1. Each commit enqueues a full-snapshot sync to the tenant's adapter (outbox in the same transaction, released only after commit). A failed sync after a narrowing leaves the member narrowed at the gateway and is drift: alert, remove extra engine access first, fence if it cannot be removed (ADR-0060 item 11; ADR-0039 item 9).
2. The verifier runs at a fixed maximum interval (proposed 15 minutes, set in the runbook), after every restore and on start. Per tenant it checks: chain and checkpoints against the anchor; `assignment == fold(history)` and `usable ⊆ granted`; every history entry's assertions and receipts against identity's and the adapter's public keys, re-validated against each entry's canonical payload as in 4.7; the catalog re-derived from the registry by the approved digest against the rows, `catalogDigest` and the anchor; `memberRoot` against `fold(history)`; rows against live memberships in identity; engine fingerprint against the snapshot at `lastVerifiedSyncVersion`; site version and generation (4.8). Any mismatch alerts and fences through `fence_notice` (5.1). The store always wins; nothing read from the engine changes the store.

### 8. Lifecycle and residency
1. **Member removal** deletes the row (5.6). History stays as audit with `memberRef` and encrypted role sets; erasure is met by removing the `memberRef`-to-person link in identity ([data-retention.md](../security/data-retention.md) line 157).
2. **Tenant deletion** first anchors the `tenant_delete` entry (5.6), then deletes `assignment`, `role_catalog` and `tenant_state`, writes the tombstone, and the assignment key is destroyed with `S10b`, leaving store history and the anchor copy unreadable but chain-verifiable. A restore from a backup taken before the deletion honours the anchored entry (4.6). Whether a readable record of role history must survive for the statutory period, under which key and with which controls, is Q-582 (blocker; legal position UNVERIFIED). ADR-0040's deprovisioning list must add this step (partner's lane, follow-up).
3. **Residency.** As 1.1. A person with memberships in two countries has unrelated `memberRef` values in two stores.

### 10. Residual risk
The write service, identity's assertion keys (customer and staff realm), the anchor and the store-state key are each country-wide targets; write service plus identity key together grant roles or catalog refreshes. Reconciliation separation of duties is weak with two founders (T52). A store, anchor or KMS outage blocks every role change in the country, and disturbing the anchor link can fence tenants (4.4). A consistent replay of an older signed state can be served by a freshly started read-path process until its first anchor-head refresh (proposed 30 seconds), and otherwise until the verifier interval if the anchor head cannot be read (6.2). The read path, the write service and the verifier each hold unwrap rights on every tenant's assignment key in the country. A removal made while the store is down can leave a tenant without an Owner (Q-583). A pre-fence signed state put back while the anchor is unreachable can lift an `apply_fenced` fence in a process that never saw the fence, until the verifier runs; it needs direct table access and grants no role. The read path's anchor credential reads heads only. Replay cannot re-ask the evaluator or past session versions, so an approval that the evaluator would have vetoed but that carries valid signed evidence replays as valid (4.7). The adapter's receipt key now also signs interim and compensation receipts; its compromise can record a refresh or revert of an already approved revision, never a new one. A tenant whose anchored chain holds a bad entry stays fenced until Q-661 is decided. None of it is tested until code exists.

## Alternatives rejected
- **Tables in the control-plane workflow database under `S22`.** Forbidden by ADR-0060 item 6.
- **Trust the action-policy evaluator's approval record.** A compromise of whatever writes it mints role changes; identity-signed assertions make the evidence independent of the evaluator.
- **Asynchronous export to the anchor.** A version could be used and then reissued after a restore; anchor-before-effect with conditional append rules that out.
- **Staff rebuild after a restore.** A free-form rebuild is a staff write (item 13); only the anchored replay is accepted.
- **Encrypt inside the database.** The key would have to reach the database; the service encrypts and the database enforces only what it can see.
- **Reuse `S10b`.** Widens a storage-and-backup key to an online service; a separate assignment key is destroyed with it.
- **Application-only tenant checks; one store spanning countries; a shared MAC for snapshots; widening at the gateway before sync.** Break ADR-0060 items 7, 14, 8 and 11.

## Consequences
- Easier: one design for ERP-008 (snapshot, receipts, drift) and PLT-003 (bootstrap, deletion); `RoleResolver` already fails closed.
- Harder: per request one database read with two signature checks and a decrypt; per role change one synchronous anchor append; per country four KMS keys (store-state, snapshot, assignment-key wrapping, verifier fence-only), an anchor account, an anchor-head refresh per read-path process and restore drills. T53 should be re-rated with this design.
- A sole-owner tenant cannot change anyone's roles (`AP-PERM-01` needs a second Owner or admin; Q-41 forbids R4 self-approval); invited members stay without a role (Q-583).
- **Blocking acceptance:** Q-581 and Q-662 (founders: the writes outside `AP-PERM-01`, now including `catalog_revert`) and Q-582 (founders and counsel: retained role history after tenant deletion). Q-580, Q-583, Q-661 and Q-663 can follow acceptance but block implementation.
- Must be done before code: a re-review of this ADR by a different model; GOV-065; contract ADRs for the signed snapshot, the signed adapter receipts (interim, final, compensation), the signed identity view and the identity approval assertion (customer and staff realm); ADR-0050 accepted (the apply path ADR-0039 item 8 relies on); SEC-002 ids for the database credentials and the four keys (store-state, snapshot, assignment-key wrapping, verifier fence-only); SEC-008 and the anchor's home; INF-001.
