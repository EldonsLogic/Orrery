# ADR-0068: A signed, single-use DDL-gate grant lets the guarded field path add a unique constraint to a new adapter-owned custom field on an allow-listed built-in engine type

Status: Proposed
Owner: partner
Approved by: pending (security review by a different model, then Eldon line by line; RULES 42 because this touches authorisation and the audit trail)

Plan ID: BPL-040 (design only; no code until this ADR is Accepted and reviewed). Answers Q-603 as the partner decided it, narrowed by the founders' answers of 2026-11-25 (item 0). Internal document: it names the engine (ADR-0016). Paths below shorten `apps/engine/change_engine/change_engine/` to `CE/`. Revision 4 re-checked every line number it cites against main at 67660816; main has moved since revision 3 (CE-SEC-008 and BPL-043 landed), so the build rows check them again. Revisions 2 to 4 answer the reviews listed under Review history.

## Context
- A Blueprint with a unique field on a built-in engine type (`customer_code` on Customer, `supplier_code` on Supplier) cannot apply. The planner marks it `gated` (`CE/adapter/engine.py:1362-1363`; `CE/adapter/frappe_backend.py:570-577`), apply then requires `approvals.gateApproved` (`CE/adapter/engine.py:1771-1779`), and the backend refuses it with `unique_or_index` before any write: `field.add` and `relation.add` at `frappe_backend.py:637-638`, a mapped `entity.add` at `:1025-1029`. `gateApproved` is only a ULID whose presence and format the adapter checks; its truth is the control plane's (`contracts/engine-adapter.md:116`, 15.3).
- The same refusal covers an existing field that lacks the constraint (`frappe_backend.py:1019-1020`), a quarantined field restored without it (`:1150-1154`) and `field.set_property` on `/unique` or `/indexed` (`:656-665`). Since BPL-043 (done), a type the adapter makes never carries `unique` or `indexed` fields, with or without a gate approval (`_refuse_constrained_fields`, `:1112-1116`, called first in `_ensure_doctype_exists`, `:1118-1119`).
- The DDL gate approves one `ADD COLUMN custom_*` statement and rejects `ADD UNIQUE` and `ADD INDEX` (`CE/guarded_apply/ddl_plan_gate.py:386-401`). For a new unique column the engine's diff yields the column with inline `unique`, then `ADD UNIQUE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS` (`:350`). `apply_native_custom_field` builds the Custom Field without `unique`, `search_index` or `length` (`CE/api/endpoints.py:1668-1691`), and the new-column predicate rejects any keyed column (`new_column_matches_plan`, `ddl_plan_gate.py:620-660`, key check `:636`).
- The guarded six-stage path runs lock, inspect and gate, durable audit intent, engine operation, reconcile, finalize (`execute_single_field_deployment`, `CE/guarded_apply/schema_reconciler.py:182`). CE-SEC-008 (done) fixed the durability defects the earlier reviews found: deployment ids are random (`frappe_backend.py:190-194`), the intent insert is strict (`endpoints.py:1385`), lookups fail closed, the baseline is durable and the lock-timeout setter raises and reads back (`endpoints.py:820-845`). CE-SEC-009 (recovery entry point) and CE-SEC-010 (deployment journal table) are in progress.
- The compiler builds only initial artifacts today: `base: None` (`services/blueprint/compiler/core.py:58-62`), and only collection-level adds become steps, so it emits no `field.add` (`services/blueprint/compiler/steps.py:62-89`). Incremental compile is BPL-039 (Eldon). The Blueprint schema already has `unique` on fields (`contracts/blueprint.schema.json:487`).
- Rules this must meet: approvals bound to tenant, exact action, data revision, approver and expiry (ADR-0027; RULES 23); identity and roles only from the authenticated session, fail closed (RULES 41); `onBehalfOf.roleIds` never authorise a configuration operation (ADP-C-012); apply is journaled and recoverable, not universally reversible (ADR-0028); adapter state lives in the tenant database (ADR-0041); control-plane credentials stay away from the adapter (ADR-0040 item 2); signing keys stay in the KMS (ADR-0038 item 2); the engine stays invisible (ADR-0016); a row depends on the other founder's row only in an earlier gate (ADR-0025).

## Decision

### 0. Founders' answers (Eldon for both founders, PR #309, 2026-11-25)
These bind every item below.
1. **Grant state: no new store.** The reservation, the deployment's idempotency row and the Stage 3 intent are written in one tenant-database transaction (item 12). `siteId` and the policy stay on the control-plane side. v1 has no stored policy floor. Withdrawing the `kid` is the emergency stop (item 4).
2. **Policy versions are plain labels.** The engine never compares policy versions as numbers; it records the label (items 2, 4).
3. **Compiler scope: unique only.** v1 covers `unique` on `field.add` only. `relation.add`, `entity.add` and index constraints are refused. Eldon owns the `constraint_change_not_isolated` compile refusal in BPL-039, and the adapter re-checks it (items 8, 11.6).

### 1. The grant
1. **Wire format, one strict parse.** Token = `ddlg1.` + base64url without padding of the payload + `.` + base64url without padding of the signature. The payload is canonical JSON (contract 5.8 rules: UTF-8, sorted keys, no whitespace), at most 2048 bytes; the whole token at most 3072 bytes. The parser rejects: duplicate keys (never last-wins), unknown or missing keys, padding or non-base64url characters, numbers with a fraction or exponent, integers outside 0 to 2^53-1, strings that miss their contract pattern (ULIDs, `art_` plus 32 lowercase hex, and every hash as `sha256:` plus 64 lowercase hex, the contract's `sha256` type, `contracts/engine-adapter/common.schema.json:9`, produced by `CE/adapter/contracts_support.py:71-74`), and a payload that does not re-canonicalise to the exact received bytes. `iat`, `exp` and `approver.authTime` are integer Unix seconds. `nonce` is 16 random bytes (22 base64url characters). The signature is 64 bytes of ed25519 over the exact payload bytes. `scope` holds exactly one entry (item 8); a second or duplicate entry refuses.
2. **Claims:** `v` (1); `typ` (`ddl_constraint_grant`); `kid`; `aud` (the adapter environment's configured audience); `tenantId`; `siteId` (item 3); `siteClass` (`sandbox`, `test`, `production`); `operation` (`apply` only); `idempotencyKey` (of the apply call); `artifactId`; `contentHash`; `baseConfigHash` (`sha256:` hash of the site export at plan time, the value the plan token binds, `CE/adapter/engine.py:1378-1388`); `gateApprovedId`; `policyVersion` (the label of the action-policy table version the issuer evaluated; a string compared by nothing in the engine, only recorded); `scope` = `[{stepSeq, kind, subject, doctype, fieldname, fieldtype, constraint}]` with `kind` `field.add` and `constraint` `unique`, the only values v1 accepts; `approver` (`kind` = `user`, `id`, `authTime`, `mfa` = true); `iat`; `exp`; `nonce`.
3. **Site incarnation.** Tenant plus site class does not identify a site: the resolver maps a tenant to a site name only. The build adds an opaque 128-bit `siteId`, minted by trusted provisioning (ADR-0040) and held in two places: the control plane's tenant-to-site record, which the issuer reads, and a one-row adapter table inside the site database, which the verifier reads. The verifier compares the grant's `siteId` with the in-site copy only, and `aud` with its own configured audience; it holds no control-plane copy. **Assumption: trusted provisioning.** Every remap, clone and restore, in place or to a new site, runs through provisioning, which mints a new id into the site copy and the control-plane record before the site serves again, so a grant issued earlier refuses afterwards. A copy made outside provisioning (an untracked clone, a manual in-place restore) keeps the old `siteId`, and nothing in the adapter can detect it; that is outside this design's threat model, and the 300 s lifetime (item 7) bounds what such a copy could replay.
4. **Issuer, and how revocation is honoured.** Only the control-plane approval service issues grants, and only at the approval's `approved` to `executing` transition in the action-policy state machine (`docs/security/action-policy-table.md:98-113`). At that moment, from its own source of truth and not from a cache, it: recomputes the change hash; refuses a cancelled, voided or expired request; re-evaluates the current gate policy row (recording its label as `policyVersion`) and the approver's and requester's current eligibility (ADR-0027, ADR-0049, ADR-0050); reads `siteId` from its tenant-to-site record; and binds the grant to the apply call's `idempotencyKey`. `approver` comes from the authenticated session's identity token, never a body; agents, support grants and service callers are refused. Revocation is honoured by refusal to issue: cancellation and voiding are allowed only while the request is pending or approved, so anything revoked before execution starts never gets a grant, and after the transition the table forbids cancellation. **Policy tightening after issue (item 0.1, 0.2).** v1 keeps no stored policy floor and the engine never orders policy versions. A policy published after a grant is issued does not stop that grant; the window is at most the 300 s lifetime plus 30 s skew. The emergency stop is withdrawing the `kid` (11.2): every unconsumed grant signed under it refuses at once. A grant not consumed before `exp` dies, and the approval ends `failed` (engine refused with no effect, table section 7); it never returns to `approved`, so running the change needs a new approval and a new grant.
5. **Key:** a new KMS ed25519 key, inventory entry "DDL-constraint grant signing key" (proposed `S24`), used through ADR-0038's `Signer`; only the approval service's workload identity may sign. The adapter holds public keys by `kid` from its environment's secret store. No private key is ever in the repo; production, staging and dev keys are separate (ADR-0018). Test vectors use a throwaway key whose private half is discarded after generation; its `kid` is refused outside tests.
6. **Why a signature, not an HMAC:** with an HMAC the verifier holds the minting secret, so a compromised adapter or engine worker could mint approvals. With a signature it can check a grant but not create one.
7. **Lifetime:** `exp - iat` at most 300 seconds, enforced by the verifier; clock skew 30 seconds. Expiry is checked again inside the reservation transaction (item 12) against the tenant database's clock, right before consumption.

### 2. The one audited code path
8. **v1 scope (item 0.3).** A grant is valid only for an artifact with **exactly one step**, a **`field.add`**, that adds **one `unique` constraint** to **one new field**. The field has no column and no metadata yet, is adapter-owned (`custom_` prefix), is Blueprint `string` or `link` (engine Data or Link), carries no `default`, has `indexed` false, and sits on a doctype in the **constraint allow-list** (a versioned data file; starts with Customer and Supplier; never a protected entity, `CE/adapter/mapping.py:249`). Why:
   - One step keeps `baseConfigHash` true from plan to effect, because no earlier step of the operation changes the export; grant, reservation, scope entry and deployment are one-to-one, so no per-field state can go stale; rollback has one inverse step. Multi-field and multi-step artifacts are refused (item 26), never partly run.
   - **Isolation is a compile refusal, re-checked by the adapter.** The constrained field must be the only change in its Blueprint revision as authored. BPL-039 (Eldon) adds the compile refusal `constraint_change_not_isolated` for a revision that adds a `unique` field next to anything else, including any `skippedOps` other than `/meta/*` and provenance. The compiler never splits a change. The adapter re-checks the same shape at 11.6 from the artifact, so an artifact from any other source cannot widen it. A Blueprint that wants a new unique field is applied as two separately approved revisions: first everything else, then the one-field constraint revision, whose `blueprint.base` must equal the first's target (`CE/adapter/engine.py:1073-1075`). Each is complete and valid alone. Until the second applies, the field is absent from the applied revision, the export, the metadata and the table, so no state has the field without its constraint. If the second fails clean, expires or loses approval, the site stays on the first revision and the constraint revision is approved again or dropped (a `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` outcome blocks the target per item 16). Anything that references the field (form, report, permission, postcondition) comes in a later revision. Studio shows the constraint revision as its own pending change after the others; its review screen shows the one field, the constraint, the type, the row count and the collation note (item 19).
   - **v1 depends on BPL-039.** Today's compiler emits no `field.add` (Context), so nothing reaches this path until incremental compile ships. `relation.add` (the schema has no `unique` on relations, `blueprint.schema.json:526`), `entity.add` and `indexed` fields are refused with or without a grant.
   - Data or Link without a default only: the gate's column definer adds `not null default 0` for numeric and Check types and a quoted default whenever one is given, then appends `unique`. Such a column fills existing rows with one value, so the unique index cannot build, and later inserts collide. Data and Link without a default get no default clause, so the column is nullable and existing rows hold NULL (MariaDB behaviour on the pinned version UNVERIFIED; bench item 24, an acceptance condition).
   - No existing-column case: `field.set_property` false to true, restoring a quarantined field that lacks the constraint, and adding a constraint to an existing native or custom field need a duplicate scan, collation-aware comparison and an existing-column reconcile predicate. v1 refuses them all (item 26).
9. All constraint DDL goes through **one function in `CE/guarded_apply/`**: a constraint mode of `execute_single_field_deployment`, reusing its stages and gate. No other caller may pass a grant; the backend receives it as an explicit argument, never from module or request state. Two existing functions change, in the constraint mode only: `apply_native_custom_field` (`endpoints.py:1668-1691`) forwards `unique` and a bounded `length` to the Custom Field it builds (it drops both today, so the engine would never emit the index); and the reconcile predicate gains the constraint variant of item 17 beside `new_column_matches_plan` (`ddl_plan_gate.py:620-660`), which today rejects any keyed column (`:636`). Outside the constraint mode both behave as today.
10. **Every other entry point refuses constraint creation.** The custom-type route is already closed by BPL-043 (done; Context). In `CE/adapter/frappe_backend.py`, `execute_step` (`:607`):
    - `field.add` reaches `_apply_or_restore_custom_field` (`:1138-1204`), the only route into the constraint mode, and only with a verified grant.
    - **The readiness check sees the grant too.** On `prepare`, before any step, the engine runs `check_ready(site.tenant_id, artifact["steps"])` with no grant (`CE/adapter/engine.py:1811-1814`), and `_check_entity_refusals` (`:846-883`) refuses a new `field.add` with `unique` (`:879-880`). So on `apply` the verified grant is also passed to `check_ready`. It lets through exactly the one allow-listed `field.add` `unique` step whose derived scope equals the grant's (11.5, 11.6) and refuses everything else as today: rollback (never given a grant), `relation.add`, `entity.add`, `indexed` and every ungranted step.
    - **No type creation on the granted path.** Today `execute_step` calls `_ensure_doctype_exists(f, doctype, {})` (`:639`) before `_apply_or_restore_custom_field` (`:640`), and that can insert and commit an adapter-owned custom type (`:1118-1136`) before any lock, check or reservation. On the granted path `check_ready` and `execute_step` both require the target to be an existing built-in type: it exists (`_doctype_exists`, `:1102-1105`) else `subject_missing`, and the adapter does not own it (`_adapter_owns_doctype`, `:1107-1110`) else `protected_subject`, both before any write. The granted path never calls `_ensure_doctype_exists`.
    - `relation.add` (same branch, `:628-640`), mapped `entity.add` (`:1025-1029`) and unmapped `entity.add` (BPL-043) keep refusing `unique` and `indexed`, with or without a grant.
    - The restore branch (`:1150-1154`), the present-field check (`:1019-1020`) and `field.set_property` on `/unique` or `/indexed` (`:656-665`) keep refusing.
    - `field.set_property` on `/default` for a field that carries an adapter-added unique constraint is refused, because a default would make repeated inserts collide.
    - The decimal branch (`:1167-1173`) runs only without `unique` or `indexed`. Rollback inverse steps run through the same `execute_step` and meet the same refusals.
11. **Checks before the effect, in this order** (any failure refuses with a neutral code, fail closed, audited per item 14). A replay of a completed idempotency key is answered first from `adapter_idempotency` by a read-only lookup (item 15). Checks 1 to 6 are pure and run before `_acquire` (`engine.py:1580`), so a refusal there neither completes the apply's idempotency key nor spends the grant; `check_ready` repeats 6 with the verified grant before any step (item 10); checks 7 to 10 run under the lock, before the reservation.
    1. Caller and tenant first, nothing read from the grant: the caller is an allow-listed service in the gateway-stamped context, and a per-tenant attempt rate on `context.tenantId` holds (configured; absent means refuse); excess refuses `rate_limited`.
    2. Strict parse and ed25519 signature (item 1) with an allow-listed, not withdrawn `kid`; `typ`, `v`, `aud` match. `iat ≤ now + skew`, `now ≤ exp`, lifetime at most 300 s. `policyVersion` is a well-formed label (non-empty, bounded) and is not compared.
    3. `tenantId` equals the authenticated `context.tenantId` and the tenant the resolver opened; `siteId` equals the in-site copy (item 3); `siteClass` equals the site's class. Only now is the attempt charged to the grant: at most 3 per (authenticated tenant, grant hash); excess refuses `rate_limited`. A refusal before this point counts only against the caller's own tenant rate, so a caller holding another tenant's grant cannot spend that tenant's budget.
    4. `artifactId`, `contentHash`, `gateApprovedId` and `idempotencyKey` equal the apply input. `baseConfigHash` equals the current export hash at admission, and again under the lock just before the reservation.
    5. Scope equality: the backend derives the one entry from the artifact and the mapping, never from the grant, and it must equal `scope` exactly.
    6. v1 shape (item 8) holds, from the artifact and live metadata: one step, `field.add`, `unique` true, `indexed` false, Data or Link, no default, allow-listed doctype that already exists and is not adapter-owned (`subject_missing`, `protected_subject`; item 10), no `skippedOps` other than `/meta/*` and provenance. This is the adapter's re-check of `constraint_change_not_isolated`.
    7. The advisory lock is held. The durable recovery and idempotency lookups succeed and find no `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` or `PREPARED` row for the target.
    8. The live DDL plan is exactly: `ADD COLUMN` of this field with no `not null` and no `default`, plus `ADD UNIQUE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS` on this column only, on this table only. No `ADD INDEX`, no `DROP`, no other column. A `MODIFY` is allowed only as an exact member of `noop_modify_allowlist(before_schema)` (`ddl_plan_gate.py:587`), the phantom no-op restatement the engine may combine with the index; any other `MODIFY` refuses before the first statement. The per-statement check before dispatch enforces the same list.
    9. Row count, bounded: `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM (SELECT 1 FROM <table> LIMIT ceiling + 1)`. No configured ceiling refuses; above it refuses with `table_too_large`. v1 needs no duplicate scan (new column only).
    10. The session `lock_wait_timeout` is set and read back through the existing raising setter (`endpoints.py:820-845`, CE-SEC-008) and re-read on the dispatching session; any other value refuses.
12. **Reservation: one tenant-database transaction (item 0.1).** One committed transaction in the tenant database (ADR-0041), on the deployment's own connection, writes three things together: (a) the reservation, unique on `(tenantId, nonce)` and `(tenantId, idempotencyKey)`, holding the grant hash, the operation binding, the single scope entry with state `reserved`, and the deployment id; (b) the deployment's idempotency row, keyed by the derived deployment id of item 13 (the CE-SEC-010 journal's unique key once it lands; the audit table's until then); (c) the Stage 3 intent of item 13. The same transaction rechecks `exp` against the database clock. Either all three commit or none does, so there is no crash window between them. The apply call's own `adapter_idempotency` row is separate: `_acquire` opens it and the apply completes it as today; the reservation binds to it through `idempotencyKey`. From the commit the grant is spent whatever the outcome; refusals at item 11 do not spend it; those after 11.3 count against its attempt budget. The scope entry then moves `reserved`, `dispatched`, and `applied`, `failed_clean` or `recovery_required`, each once. A clone or restore of the tenant database rolls the reservation back with everything else; item 3 rotates `siteId` on those, so an earlier grant still refuses.
13. **Durable evidence before any effect.** The deployment id is `dep_` plus the first 32 hex characters of sha256(`tenantId|nonce|stepSeq|unique`), so a resume finds the same deployment. The intent is the strict `INSERT` CE-SEC-008 introduced: a duplicate key never continues to the engine operation. With a matching binding (`DeploymentIntentConflictError("already_recorded")`, `CE/api/endpoints.py:1601-1617`) the deployment returns its stored finalized outcome if one exists, else it is held as `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` (`intent_already_recorded`, `CE/guarded_apply/schema_reconciler.py:387-405`) for reconcile per item 18; with any other binding it refuses (`intent_binding_mismatch`) with no effect. The grant is spent from the item 12 commit, so a resume never re-runs DDL. The committed intent holds the complete before-schema fingerprint (every key in `SCHEMA_KEYS`, `ddl_plan_gate.py:528`), the approved statements, the expected delta (column definition and index descriptor), the reservation id and the grant hash. A dispatch journal row is committed immediately before each statement is sent and updated when it returns. Reconcile reads only this durable evidence; a failed lookup blocks (`engine_unavailable`). If any write fails, nothing runs.
14. **Audit record** (internal only, `contracts/engine-adapter.md:200`): auditId, requestId, correlationId, tenant, siteId, siteClass, aud, caller service, `onBehalfOf` (recorded, not trusted), `kid`, nonce, sha256 of the grant (never the grant), approver id, `authTime`, `iat`, `exp`, `policyVersion` (as a label), `gateApprovedId`, idempotencyKey, artifactId, contentHash, reservation id, deployment id, scope entry, row count, lock timeout read back, approved statements, dispatch journal, before and after fingerprints, outcome and stage timings. Every refusal writes a refusal record (grant hash, code, stage), capped per authenticated tenant and, after 11.3, per grant hash.
15. **Replay.** The same idempotency key and input returns the stored outcome without a new effect, before any grant check, so an expired grant does not turn a finished apply into a refusal. The same key with another grant refuses `idempotency_conflict`. A failed lookup blocks; it never counts as "no earlier attempt".

### 3. Failure and rollback
16. **A failed statement does not prove `failed_clean`.** After any failure, and on restart, the outcome comes only from the durable baseline and a fresh complete schema read: `failed_clean` only if the live schema equals the baseline on every fingerprint key and no engine metadata row exists for the field; `applied` only per item 17; anything else, including an incomplete or failed read, is `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`. Refusal mapping: a refusal in Stage 2 (before the reservation) and a verified `failed_clean` raise `engine_unavailable` with `side_effect_free=True`, so the apply ends without forcing `recovery_required` (today the backend raises them with the default `False`, `frappe_backend.py:1200-1203`, which the engine turns into `recovery_required`, `engine.py:1869`); only an unverified outcome stays `side_effect_free=False`. Whether a failed or interrupted `ALTER TABLE` leaves the table unchanged on the pinned MariaDB version is UNVERIFIED; bench item 24.
17. **New-column predicate only.** The constraint variant of the reconcile predicate accepts exactly: the baseline plus the approved column at the end of the column order (approved type, nullable, default NULL, the table's default charset and collation, key `UNI`), plus exactly one unique index matching the full approved descriptor (name, uniqueness, the single column, no prefix length, BTREE) and its UNIQUE row in the constraints fingerprint, with every other column, index, constraint, check, trigger and table attribute unchanged except the allowed phantom `MODIFY` of 11.8 (which leaves the definition unchanged), and engine metadata matching the approved definition including `unique`. No existing-column predicate exists because v1 has no existing-column case (item 8).
18. **Crash between DDL and finalize.** Reservation, intent, baseline and dispatch journal are already durable (item 12). On restart the deployment is `PREPARED`, the target stays blocked, and reconcile classifies it per items 16 and 17 (the CE-SEC-009 recovery entry point once it lands). A missing or unreadable piece of evidence gives `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`, never `applied`. Recovery never re-runs DDL; the reservation is spent.
19. **Rollback keeps the constraint.** The inverse of the add is quarantine, which hides the field and keeps column and index (`frappe_backend.py:1206-1209`; `contracts/engine-adapter.md:31`, `:133`). Later inserts must write NULL, not `''`, into the hidden column, and so must blank inserts while the field is live; both UNVERIFIED, bench item 24. With index constraints out of v1 there is no fallback: if the bench shows `''`, this ADR is not accepted as written. Uniqueness follows the column collation, expected to be case-insensitive (`ABC` equals `abc`; UNVERIFIED); the review screen tells the approver. Re-applying restores through the restore branch, which needs no DDL because the index still exists. Dropping a constraint stays refused. `check_ready` already refuses `unique_or_index` before any step (`_check_entity_refusals`, from BPL-041), and rollback never passes it a grant (item 10), so a rollback that would need constraint DDL is refused before any step runs.

### 4. What stays refused
20. Everything outside item 8 keeps `unique_or_index`: any step without a valid grant or with a mismatched scope; `relation.add` and `entity.add` with a constrained field; every `indexed` field; artifacts with more than one step or more than one constrained field; a revision that adds a constrained field next to any other change; existing columns (native such as `item_code`, earlier custom, quarantined restore, `set_property` in either direction); types other than Data and Link; fields with a default; constraint fields in a new custom type; doctypes off the allow-list; composite or prefix indexes; tables above the ceiling; and every rollback that would drop a constraint.

### 5. Threats and abuse cases
Tests are named `test_bpl_040_<name>`; `bench:` runs on real MariaDB.

## Alternatives rejected
- A controlled admin deployment only: no binding to the approved change, a second unaudited path, does not scale. It stays the fallback above the ceiling.
- Adding the column without the constraint: rejected in the BPL-031 review (the Blueprint would apply without enforcing uniqueness).
- An HMAC grant: the verifier could mint (item 6).
- The adapter fetching the approval record by ULID at run time: a new runtime dependency and trust channel into the engine side. Issuing at the executing transition gets the same freshness offline.
- A grant valid for a whole multi-field, multi-step artifact with per-field scope entries and intermediate-state checks: needs a schema prediction per step and per-field recovery. Rejected for v1; revisit with evidence of need.
- A compiler split into an ordinary and a constraint artifact: two authored, separately approved revisions reach the same outcome with no half state (item 8).
- A dedicated regional grant store holding the reservation, a `siteId` binding and a policy floor, outside the tenant database: contradicts ADR-0041 and ADR-0040 item 2 and adds a store, a principal and a cross-store crash window. Rejected by the founders (item 0.1).
- A stored, monotonic policy floor with integer policy versions: rejected by the founders (item 0.1, 0.2); the 300 s window and `kid` withdrawal bound the risk.
- Index constraints, `relation.add` and `entity.add` in v1: no compiler path emits them and the schema has no relation flags; deferred by the founders (item 0.3).
- Existing-column cases in v1: need duplicate scans, collation handling and a second reconcile predicate. Deferred (item 8).
- Trusting `approvals.gateApproved` as it stands: an unsigned id with no scope, approver or expiry.

## Consequences
- Easier: Blueprints with unique customer or supplier codes apply on built-in types, with an approval that is bound, single-use, revocation-aware and audited, and with no new store.
- Harder: a KMS key, a contract field, an issuer at the executing transition, incremental compile (BPL-039), an in-site `siteId` and the one-transaction reservation must exist first. The owner approves a constrained field as a separate change after the rest. Provisioning must rotate `siteId` on every remap, clone and restore. A policy tightened after issue does not stop a live grant for up to 330 s unless the `kid` is withdrawn. Indexed fields stay refused.
- Open for founders: the initial allow-list (Customer and Supplier proposed); the row ceiling and attempt budgets (set after a bench measurement); whether the approver must differ from the requester for this gate; MFA step-up for production grants (ADR-0047); who owns Studio's display of the separate change and the `siteId` rotation in remap, clone and restore tooling.
