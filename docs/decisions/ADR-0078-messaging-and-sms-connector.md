# ADR-0078: Messaging and SMS connector: one conversation model for Meta's channels plus SMS, untrusted inbound, drafts by default

Status: Proposed
Owner: eldon
Approved by: pending (Eldon proposer; partner review on this PR). Touches contracts, tenancy and PDPL: Tier 3, needs a security review by a different model.

## Context
[ADR-0076](ADR-0076-phase-1-module-scope.md) (Proposed) adds an omnichannel inbox (WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook) and marketing to phase 1 and asks for a messaging and SMS connector contract first (DSC-022). [OPR-015](../product/opr-015-messaging-channel.md) already designs WhatsApp as a *staff* channel into the chat; this is the other direction: a tenant's customers writing to the business. It brings a new processor (Meta, an SMS provider), untrusted text at volume, and sends that cost money and reach people outside the tenant.

Platform facts (Meta developer docs, accessed 2026-11-27):
- WhatsApp: a user message or call starts a 24-hour customer service window, reset by each new one; inside it any service message may be sent; when it closes only pre-approved templates may be sent ([send messages](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/messages/send-messages)). The send response means accepted, not delivered; status arrives by webhook (sent, delivered, read, failed); order is not guaranteed; no delivered status before the time-to-live (30 days by default) means dropped (same page).
- Templates are `authentication`, `marketing` or `utility`; only `APPROVED` templates can be sent ([template guidelines](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/message-templates/guidelines)).
- Opt-in before business-initiated messages: the person shares a number and agrees to messages from a named business; the method is the business's choice within local law; Meta recommends opt-in per category and clear opt-out ([getting opt-in](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/overview/getting-opt-in)). That page names no record-keeping duty; we keep records anyway.
- Messenger and Instagram: a 24-hour standard messaging window for most user actions (up to 7 days after a click-to-message ad); a Human Agent tag allows manual replies for 7 days; tags outside approved use can lead to restrictions ([Messenger policy overview](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform/policy/policy-overview)).
- Webhooks (accessed 2026-11-27, partner's read of the pages for Q-721): `X-Hub-Signature-256` carries a SHA-256 signature with a `sha256=` prefix, keyed by the App Secret, over the raw body; no signed timestamp or nonce is mentioned, read as none ([webhooks getting started](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/webhooks/getting-started), "Validating Payloads"). That it is HMAC-SHA256 over the raw body is the usual reading but UNVERIFIED as wording. WhatsApp retries "for up to 7 days" and every subscribed app is retried, so duplicates happen ([set up webhooks](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/guides/set-up-webhooks), "Webhook delivery failure"); the generic Graph API page says 36 hours ("Frequency"), and which one Meta applies in practice is UNVERIFIED. Per-message `messages[].timestamp` and `statuses[].timestamp` exist as strings ([webhook components](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/webhooks/components), "Payload structures"); that they are unix seconds is UNVERIFIED.
- Messenger webhooks (accessed 2026-11-27, [Messenger webhooks](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/webhooks)): `X-Hub-Signature-256` with a `sha256=` prefix; example `time` and `timestamp` are unquoted 13-digit numbers (unit not stated, UNVERIFIED); a failed delivery is retried a few times, an alert follows after 15 minutes and the app is unsubscribed after 1 hour. The Instagram webhooks page ([Instagram webhooks](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/webhooks), accessed 2026-11-27) states no timestamp format, retry period or signature header, so Instagram is blocked (Q-724).
- UNVERIFIED (not read at an official source): Meta business verification steps and lead times; whether service-window replies are charged; per-country WhatsApp rates; Meta data residency and processor terms; SMS sender-id registration and marketing-SMS rules in KSA, UAE, Egypt and Qatar; cross-border transfer rules under each market's data law (LEG-003).

## Decision
1. **One conversation model** across WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger (Meta's business platform) plus one provider-agnostic SMS port: [contracts/connector-messaging.md](../../contracts/connector-messaging.md) 0.1.0 and its schema. Channel differences live in a capability declaration, not in callers. Distinct from OPR-015: customer contacts are never principals and never run tools.
2. **Inbound is always untrusted** (L3, PI-RULE-1 to 5, AGENTS.md security rules). Sanitised, flagged, labelled at the boundary; any thread with inbound text is tainted; links and media are not fetched or read in v1.
3. **Replies are drafts by default.** An agent drafts into the inbox; a human sends. Every thread with inbound text is tainted (prompt-injection-defence 4.5), so every human or agent-drafted send executes only on a confirmation gesture over the canonical payload; the send records `sentBy`, `draftedBy` (agent id), `confirmationId` and `payloadHash`.
4. **Auto-reply** is a per-tenant opt-in limited to a fixed list of safe intents (greeting, opening hours, location, hand-off), inside the service window, with fixed tenant-approved texts named by `approvedTextId` under an `autoReplyPolicyRef` (the send carries no text; the model only classifies, and `classifiedIntent`, `confidence` and a `confidenceThreshold` of at least 0.8 are recorded), Meta channels only, one per intent per window and daily caps (placeholders, Q-156). This is an agent-initiated external send, which the action-policy table today forbids ("draft-only, everywhere"). So it stays off until a SEC-owned action-policy row and its security review allow it, **and** a floor ADR amends ADR-0027 and action-policy section 9 ("draft-only, everywhere") for this one no-gesture send; this ADR does not change the table or the human floor (Q-720, DSC-024). Each auto-reply names the inbound message it answers (`inboundMessageId`), uses the policy's threshold, and is refused on any flag or a second reply in the same window.
5. **Outbound marketing only through approved templates**, to contacts with a granted marketing consent recorded per contact, channel, country and purpose as append-only records (latest wins; the send carries `country`); opt-out (STOP and Arabic equivalents, in-app, provider block) revokes at once, in every purpose and phase-1 country on its channel (the message has no country), and may be the first record for a key; a contact's revoke stands across the whole chain until the contact's own opt-in names it, never a tenant user, and a tenant revoke cannot be stacked over it; a keyword opt-in counts only from a signed, dated callback. Marketing sends are R3, human-initiated with a non-null `approvalId`; never agent-creatable.
6. **Sending is a Tool API write**, proposed R3 for every send until the SEC row exists (Q-720): action-policy section 4 gives R1 no external effect, and a message to a customer is one. Marketing and any batch are R3 with an approval; idempotency key required; recipient only by contact record id (prompt-injection-defence 5.1).
7. **Two secrets in the secret store**: `tokenRef` (send token, per tenant, send worker only) and `webhookSecretRef` (S13, platform or tenant until Q-695, receiving endpoint only); a channel account binds to exactly one tenant; inbound resolves the tenant only from the verified account id.
8. **Webhooks verified** (signature before parsing, fail closed), **idempotent** (dedup key per provider event) and **replay-protected** by dedup on the provider event id, kept at least 192 hours (WhatsApp's 7-day retry period plus a 24 h margin) and never less than the event age limit, plus a payload age limit (`maxEventAgeHours`, 37 to 192, default 192 for WhatsApp) because the signature carries no timestamp; the per-message timestamp is parsed as a string and fails closed unless 10 or 13 digits; timestamp format, retry period and age are fixed per connector (WhatsApp string, 168 h retry, age 192; Messenger 13-digit number, 1 h retry); dedup retention is at least the age limit plus 1 hour, counted from first receipt; the verification method is recorded, and a path-secret call never grants consent; status webhooks are deduplicated on message, status and time; an opt-out keyword is always processed, and an old STOP never ends a later grant (compared on `providerSentAt`); an unknown event type fails closed and is audited (MSG-013).
9. **Minimise personal data**: sealed numbers plus keyed hash, opaque refs in objects, no bodies in events; message text and params are PD and C3 until classified (unknown is C3), stored in the tenant's region; retention per market from the country pack, with no expiry set yet (D-10 open); production is held until LEG-003 and LEG-012 are answered.
10. **Customer go-live items, not build items**: each customer's Meta business verification, display name, template approvals per language, and SMS sender registrations (ADR-0076 decision 11). Build and tests use provider test numbers and synthetic data only.
11. Events 0.3.0 (following the 0.2.0 payment types of ADR-0077) adds `message.*`, `consent.*` and `meter.message_sent` (additive).

## Alternatives rejected
- A separate model per channel: duplicates consent, audit and inbox logic three times and lets rules drift.
- Reuse OPR-015's staff binding for customers: would give a customer number a principal and a tool menu.
- Model-written auto-replies: a tainted model send to an outside person with no human gesture; breaks the action-policy table and PI rules.
- Marketing by agents, or without per-purpose consent: floor breach and a PDPL and Meta policy risk.
- Pick one SMS vendor now: the four markets differ on sender registration (UNVERIFIED); a port defers the choice to DSC-024.

## Consequences
- Easier: one inbox screen (WEB-035) and marketing contract (BPL-055) build on one set of objects; contract tests guard the rules JSON Schema can express.
- Harder: auto-reply needs an action-policy row first; marketing waits on counsel per market; Meta adds a sub-processor and cost line.
- Must do: DSC-024 (build, test numbers only); a SEC action-policy row for `message.*` and auto-reply; LEG-003 per-market transfer and marketing-consent answers; country packs carry the messaging retention period.
- Open points: Q-695 (own Meta app or ours), Q-696 (Cloud API or BSP, SMS provider per market), Q-697 (Messenger and Instagram tags in v1), Q-698 (replay window, auto-reply caps with Q-156, owner of the action-policy row), Q-720 (risk class for a non-financial external send), Q-721 (Meta signed timestamp and dedup retention), Q-724 (Instagram webhook facts), Q-725 (does a STOP cover the contact's other channels).
